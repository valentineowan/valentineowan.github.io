Bachelor of Education research project · 2018

Conflict Management Strategies and Secondary School Teachers’ Job Effectiveness in Obubra Local Government Area of Cross River State, Nigeria

This undergraduate research examined how arbitration, dialogue, effective communication and smoothing relate to the job effectiveness of secondary school teachers in Obubra Local Government Area, Cross River State.

Bachelor of Education (B.Ed.) in Educational Administration and Planning · University of Calabar · September 2018

Read project (PDF) Research record Back to all projects

Plain-language summary

Conflicts can arise in schools when staff members disagree about responsibilities, decisions, communication, resources or how work should be done. The way school leaders respond to these disagreements may affect teachers’ ability to carry out their duties effectively. This project examined four strategies for managing conflict in secondary schools: arbitration, dialogue, effective communication and smoothing. It focused on public secondary school teachers in Obubra Local Government Area of Cross River State, Nigeria.

The study was conducted during the 2017/2018 academic session. It used correlational and factorial research designs and included 222 teachers drawn from a population of 352. Ten public secondary schools were selected to form the sampling frame, and teachers in the selected schools were included through purposive sampling. Data were collected with two researcher-designed questionnaires: the Conflict Management Strategies Questionnaire (CMSQ) and the Secondary School Teachers’ Job Effectiveness Questionnaire (SSTJEQ). The instruments were validated by experts, and their internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha. The reported reliability coefficients were .85 for arbitration, .73 for dialogue, .83 for effective communication, .86 for smoothing and .87 for teachers’ job effectiveness.

The results indicated that teachers’ job effectiveness in the study area was significantly high. Arbitration, dialogue and effective communication each had a statistically significant relationship with teachers’ job effectiveness. Smoothing, considered on its own, did not have a statistically significant relationship with job effectiveness. When the four strategies were considered jointly, however, they had a significant combined influence, with effective communication showing the greatest influence among the strategies examined.

The central practical message is that school leaders should not assume that one conflict management approach will be suitable for every disagreement. Arbitration may be useful when an impartial decision is needed; dialogue can help parties explain their concerns and seek understanding; and effective communication can clarify expectations and reduce misunderstandings. Smoothing may have a place in particular circumstances, but the findings of this study did not show a significant individual relationship between smoothing and teachers’ job effectiveness. Principals should therefore consider the nature of each conflict and use an appropriate combination of strategies to support constructive working relationships and teachers’ performance.

Abstract

The study investigated conflict management strategies and secondary school teachers’ job effectiveness in Obubra Local Government Area of Cross River State. Six null hypotheses were formulated to guide the study. The study adopted correlational and factorial research designs. Purposive sampling technique was used to select a sample of 222 teachers from a population of 352 secondary school teachers. Conflict Management Strategies Questionnaire (CMSQ) and Secondary School Teachers’ Job Effectiveness Questionnaire (SSTJEQ) were used respectively, as instruments for data collection. The hypotheses were tested at .05 level of significance using Population t-test, Pearson Product Moment Correlation and Multiple Regression analyses. Findings revealed that, teachers’ job effectiveness level in Obubra Local Government Area is significantly high. Findings also revealed that arbitration, dialogue, and effective communication strategies respectively, had a significant relationship with secondary school teachers’ job effectiveness. Smoothing strategy had no significant relationship to secondary school teachers’ job effectiveness. The findings also revealed among others that; the four conflict management strategies (arbitration, dialogue, effective communication and smoothing) had a joint significant influence on secondary school teachers’ job effectiveness. Based on these findings, it was recommended among others that; secondary school principals should not rely totally on one conflict management strategy as the best for all situations, instead they should learn how to use various conflict management strategies, and apply them in any given conflict situation in their schools.
